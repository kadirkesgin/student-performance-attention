\begin{abstract}
Predicting student academic outcomes requires models that are not only accurate but also scientifically rigorous and interpretable. This study evaluates a Hybrid Attention-based model against state-of-the-art ensemble methods across three heterogeneous educational datasets, with a specific focus on behavior-sensitive prediction. After explicitly removing target-leaking features (e.g., ExamScore), our proposed model demonstrates a significant accuracy boost of approximately 6\% through self-attention mechanisms in behavioral datasets (Kaggle 2024), outperforming traditional Gradient Boosting (+34\% relative) and its own non-attention ablation variant. Empirical SHAP analysis reveals that behavioral indicators such as \textit{Attendance} and \textit{Stress Level} are the primary predictive drivers, establishing the model's suitability for early-warning systems where direct grades are unavailable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Materials and Methods}
We utilized three real-world datasets, ensuring rigorous 5-fold stratified cross-validation and the exclusion of target-leaking variables:
\begin{itemize}
    \item UCI Dropout and Success Dataset (Institutional)
    \item Mendeley AI Course Dataset (Course-specific)
    \item Kaggle Learning Behavior 2024 (Behavioral-centric)
\end{itemize}
